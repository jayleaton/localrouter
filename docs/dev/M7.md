# M7: MiniMax H3 text to video + audio, the plan

What ComfyUI 0.37.0 does is in `tools/twin/VIDEO-PORT.md` (file:line references). This file is what LocalRouter builds
and how it is checked. Same method as the image tool: a Python twin that runs every op on a kernel LocalRouter owns,
gated against the original; the Zig engine bit-exact with the twin, op by op and end to end.

## The reference ("the video twin")

ComfyUI's MiniMax H3 text-to-video pipeline, op for op (packing, the curve time embedding, per-token modulation, RoPE,
the audio carry, `res_multistep` with eta 0 on the `simple` schedule, the VAEs, the frame and sample conversions), with
these declared substitutions (each one is where ComfyUI's numerics depend on a backend or on history):

| Part | ComfyUI | The twin (and the engine) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noise | CPU `mt19937` `randn`, video then audio | LocalRouter's portable generator (`noise.py`), video then audio, same layout | portable across machines; ComfyUI's draw depends on the CPU's vector path |
| DiT linears | bf16 (stock) or tfvideo NVFP4 with *delayed* input scales | TensorFold NVFP4 W4A4 with **static** calibrated input scales; FP8 later | delayed scales make a run depend on the previous forward |
| DiT attention | SDPA (stock) / comfy-kitchen INT8 (tfvideo) / sol-attn on steps 2+ (workflow) | comfy-kitchen INT8 attention, copied as source, every step dense | the shipping speed path; sparse attention is a later speed lever with its own gate |
| q/k norm + RoPE | `comfy_kitchen.rms_rope_split_half_` | the same kernel, copied | already a fixed kernel |
| Block elementwise | bf16 torch ops (stock) / tfvideo Triton | our CUDA kernels with tfvideo's arithmetic (fp32 inside, one rounding) | no Triton in the engine for video |
| fp32 islands (patch projections, curve modulation, final heads), refiner, text attention | torch fp32 / bf16 / SDPA | our deterministic fp32 and bf16 GEMMs and attention | cuBLAS / SDPA picks kernels by shape |
| Text encoder | NVFP4 AWQ weights dequantized to fp32, cuBLAS SGEMM, fp32 SDPA | the same dequantization, our fp32 GEMM and attention, fp32 throughout | as above |
| Video VAE | fp16 ViT (stock) or int8 convrot (workflow), tiled | the fp16 path on our fp16 GEMM and attention, the same tiling and blends | the fp16 file is the unquantized one |
| Audio VAE | fp32 BigVGAN on cuDNN (TF32 allowed for convolutions) | fp32 on our kernels, no TF32 | TF32 is a backend choice |
| Sampler scalars | CUDA `log` / `expm1` / `exp` on 0-d tensors | portable f64 math rounded to f32, shared by twin and engine | portable |
| mp4 | PyAV, libx264 defaults, AAC defaults | ffmpeg; the gate compares decoded frames (uint8) and audio samples before encoding | encoders are not bit-reproducible across builds |

Gates against ComfyUI (the twin is "the same model"): per-step velocity cosine of the twin's DiT vs stock ComfyUI's
bf16 DiT on the same inputs, against the floor two ComfyUI attention backends give each other; text encoder output
cosine vs ComfyUI's; VAE PSNR vs ComfyUI's decode of the same latents; audio SNR; and whole clips scored as tfvideo
did (LPIPS, DINO, log-mel) at 768x448, 2 s.

Gates of the engine against the twin: per op alone and chained (`localrouter check h3-replay`), then whole clips: the uint8
frames and the fp32 audio samples byte-equal, on sm_120; speed no slower than the twin.

## Full clips (5 s) and memory

The tool serves up to 768x448 and 5 s: `max_seconds` 5 x 24 fps = 120 frames, aligned up to 17 k + 5 = **124 frames**. The
numbers below are computed from `layout.zig`, `pipeline.zig`, `dit.zig` and the VAEs' sources, scaled from the GB10 run
of 2026-10-07 (56 frames); `stk check h3-bench` (below) replaces every estimate with a measurement.

**Shapes.**

| | 56 frames (2.3 s) | 124 frames (5.2 s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| latent frames T = ((F - 5) / 17) * 5 + 2 | 17 | 37 |
| latent grid (height / 16 x width / 16), 2x2 patches | 28 x 48, 14 x 24 = 336 per frame | same |
| video tokens, T x 336 | 5,712 | 12,432 |
| audio tokens, 2 x A, A = round(F / 24 * 40) | 186 (A = 93) | 414 (A = 207) |
| text tokens L (the check's prompt 69; `Limits.tokens` 512) | 69 (cap 512) | 69 (cap 512) |
| sequence S = L + 2 A + video | **5,967** (cap 6,410) | **12,915** (cap 13,358) |
| uint8 frames (device and host) | 57.8 MB | 128.0 MB |

The sampler state is [video | audio] fp32: 24 T 28 48 + 64 A = 1.21 M floats at 124 frames (4.8 MB a buffer); nothing
here grows with the clip but the frames.

**Time (scaling from the 56-frame warm run: encode 0.96 s, 8 steps 13.96 s, audio 0.09 s, video 14.06 s).**

- Encode: the prompt only: 1 s, unchanged. The text refiner is the DiT's `prepare`, also independent of F.
- A DiT step: the NVFP4 linears and the elementwise ops are proportional to S (x 2.16 from 5,967 to 12,915); the INT8
  attention to S^2 (x 4.68). The 1.74 s step splits between them unmeasured; with attention at 20 to 35 % of the step:
  1.74 x (0.8 x 2.16 + 0.2 x 4.68) = 4.7 s up to 1.74 x (0.65 x 2.16 + 0.35 x 4.68) = 5.3 s a step, **37 to 43 s for 8
  steps** (the linears alone: 30 s; all attention: 65 s, the bounds). The host's RoPE table is built once a request on
  the first step (S x 144 sincos, ms).
- Audio VAE: proportional to A (x 2.2): 0.2 s.
- Video VAE: temporal clips of 7 latent frames every 5, each decoded in 8 spatial tiles (2 x 4 at 448 x 768) of
  1,797 tokens: `temporalChunks(37)` = 7 clips against 3 at T = 17 (x 2.33): **33 s**. It is now as long as the sampling.
- Warm total about **71 to 77 s** for a 5.2 s clip (against 29 s for 2.3 s), plus ffmpeg's H.264 and AAC (seconds) and the
  load (14 s, once; with `unload_te` about 7 s more per request, the encoder's share of 32.8 GB).

**Memory (the engine; `Pipeline.create` allocates every scratch buffer up front for `Limits`, so a request pays nothing
at run time except the VAE canvases).**

| | 56 frames | 124 frames |
| --- | --- | --- |
| weights, measured on GB10 | 32.8 GB | 32.8 GB |
| DiT scratch: 271 KB x S_max (h, hn, qkv, att, o, gu, act: 10.7 k + 10.7 k + 43 k + 14 k + 14 k + 10.7 k + 57 k + 28.7 k B a token; fp8 and scale rows, fp32 embeddings, the INT8 attention workspace) | 1.65 GiB (S_max 6,410) | **3.4 GiB** (S_max 13,358) |
| text encoder scratch (512 tokens, fp32) | 0.2 GiB | 0.2 GiB |
| video VAE tile scratch (one 256 px tile: scores 207 MB, FFN 90 MB, qkv 22 MB, ...) | 0.33 GiB | 0.33 GiB |
| video VAE per decode: two clip canvases 2 x 55 MiB, eight tile buffers 84 MiB, latents | 0.2 GiB | 0.2 GiB |
| audio VAE scratch (`col` 140,800 x A x 4, activations 2 x 800 A x 8) | 0.16 GiB | 0.3 GiB |
| pipeline buffers (state, bf16 in/out, text, fp16 latents, uint8 frames) | 0.09 GiB | 0.15 GiB |
| scratch total | 2.6 GiB | **4.6 GiB** |
| **resident** (weights + scratch + context) | about 35.8 GB | **about 38 GB** |

So a 5 s clip costs 2 GiB more than a 2.3 s one, and everything fits a 128 GB GB10 three times over. The 93 GiB the last
window reported was MemAvailable with the twin generating in the same window (`gpu_used_mib` 117,757): not the engine's.
`pipeline.zig`'s old memory note (DiT scratch 10 GB at "38 k tokens", all resident 60 GB) and the tool's
`default_resident_mb` 64,000 are both too high (S is 6 k at 56 frames, not 38 k): corrected in the note, the tool's
default is left to its owner (it reserves about 26 GB that the engine never touches).

**Phases.** There is no phase-dependent peak: the scratch is allocated at `create`, the weights never leave, so the
live set is the resident figure from the end of `create` to `destroy`; the only transient is the VAE's 0.2 GiB of
canvases during a decode (and the uploader's pinned staging while loading). With `unload_te`:

- between requests, and while sampling and decoding: the encoder's weights and 0.2 GiB scratch are gone (about 16 GB: 32.8
  GB less the DiT and the VAEs, which the bench prints by component);
- **the peak does not change**: `generate` loads the encoder first, with the DiT, its scratch and both VAEs already
  resident (`create` allocates them regardless), so the encode runs at the full resident figure. `unload_te` buys
  headroom for a second tool between requests and during the 70 s of sampling and decoding, not a lower peak, and costs
  the checkpoint's re-read every request. Lowering the peak would take freeing the VAEs' and the DiT's scratch during the
  encode, or not allocating the DiT's until after it: not done.

**Limits for 124 frames.** `Limits{ .width = 768, .height = 448, .frames = 120 or 124 (both align to 124), .tokens = 512 }`
is what the tool already passes (`max_seconds` 5): `max_s` = 512 + 2 x 207 + 37 x 14 x 24 = 13,358 sizes `Dit.init`
(`h`, `qkv`, `gu`, the quantizer rows, the INT8 workspace) once, `p.max.audio_t` = 207 the audio VAE, `p.max.frame_bytes`
the uint8 buffer. A request of any smaller size or length fits (`generate` refuses a larger one with `RequestTooLarge`,
the DiT with `VideoTooLong`). The production process therefore always carries the 124-frame figure, whatever it serves;
`h3-bench` sizes Limits to its own request, so its 56-frame run shows the 56-frame resident, not the tool's.

**What may break at 124 frames (read from the code; no GPU here).**

1. The INT8 attention at S = 12.9 k to 13.4 k: `test_kitchen` (bitwise against the wheel) covered S = 80, 4,096 and 9,000, and
   the gate only 5,967. Nothing in the launch code limits S (workspace sized by `Kitchen.Plan.of`), but a clip at 124
   frames has no twin capture: a determinism check (`equal_runs` in the bench) and a look at the MP4 are the evidence, a
   twin run at 124 frames the proof.
2. Limits that are not reached: the quantizer's grid.y is the padded row count (<= 65,535: S would have to pass
   65 k, 5x this); the largest activation (`gu`, S x 28,672) is 383 M elements against 2^31 where a kernel indexes with
   `int` (S 75 k); `n_video` 1.2 M. None breaks below about 5x the tokens, so a longer or larger clip, not this one.
3. The video VAE's memory and tile plan do not depend on F (the clip count does: time, not size); `FrameCountMismatch`
   is checked in `decode` (`outputFrames(37)` = 7 x 17 + 5 = 124, equal to the request's).
4. Bit-exactness at 124 frames is untested: the noise stream, the sampler state, and the audio length (207 x 800 samples,
   5.175 s, the clip 5.167 s, muxed with `-shortest`) follow the twin's formulas but no capture has them.
5. The tool: `needs.resident` 64 GB is 26 GB above the engine's figure (admission over-reserves); the host copies of frames
   and waveform (128 MB + 1.3 MB) plus ffmpeg's are small; the job timeout (3,600 s) leaves a 75 s clip far inside.

**`stk check h3-bench PACK TE_CKPT VAE_AUDIO VAE_VIDEO [--size 768x448] [--frames N] [--steps 8] [--unload-te] [--runs 2] [--out PATH.mp4]`**
(`src/cli/h3_bench.zig`): the engine alone, no capture and no twin. It creates the pipeline with Limits sized for the
request (tokens 512, the tool's), generates `--runs` clips from one prompt and seed, and prints one JSON line:
the shapes (`seq_tokens` by part), `load_ms`, `weights` by component, `first_ms` and `warm_ms` (encode, sample, audio, video,
total), `mem` (MemAvailable every 50 ms on a thread: start, min, `peak_drop_mib`, the running drop after the load and the
first run; `cuMemGetInfo` used MiB before, after the load and after the runs, which on GB10 is the system's), the sha256 of
the frames and the waveform, `equal_runs`, and with `--out` the MP4 (written after the sampler stops; `mp4_error` when
ffmpeg is missing). With `--unload-te` the encoder's weights read 0 (it is gone when the weights are counted) and every
run's encode includes its reload.
